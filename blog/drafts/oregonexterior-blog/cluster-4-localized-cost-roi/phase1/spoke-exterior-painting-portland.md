## The Full Return Calculation

Resale recovery is one column. Here are the other four.

### 1. Structural-Damage Avoidance

This is the biggest number most homeowners never see — because it only becomes visible when you don't maintain.

Portland sits on Portland Hills Silt over Missoula Flood deposits, with Boring Lava basalt in the east hills. The silt retains moisture. The clay fraction swells. When your siding fails — when paint cracks, caulk separates, or lapping joints open up — moisture enters the wall cavity through capillary action.

The physics: water moves through a failed paint film at approximately 0.3 mm/hour via capillary wicking. Portland's wet season runs roughly 2,400 hours (October through May). Even if your siding is only failing on one elevation, that's enough moisture migration to saturate the sheathing behind it within one season.

Once sheathing is saturated:
- **Fungal colonization begins within 72 hours** at Oregon's shoulder-season temperatures (45–60°F).
- **Sub-fascia connections soften.** The structural tie between your roof system and wall framing degrades.
- **Insulation loses R-value.** Wet fiberglass insulation performs at roughly 40% of rated capacity. Your heating bill goes up.
- **Flashing fails at the siding-to-trim transition.** Water bypasses the weather-resistive barrier entirely.

**Cost of structural damage from 5 years of siding deferral in Portland:** $15,000–$22,000 in sheathing replacement, framing repair, insulation replacement, and interior finish restoration. We've seen it. Repeatedly.

A $10,000 exterior repaint prevents $15,000–$22,000 in damage. That's not maintenance. That's arbitrage.

### 2. Energy Savings

Failed siding is a failed thermal envelope. Every crack, gap, and paint failure is an air leak. Air leakage is the single largest energy loss mechanism in residential buildings — larger than insulation quality, window performance, or HVAC efficiency.

The 2026 ORSC requires continuous air barriers at the sheathing plane for any project that involves significant exterior work. When you repaint or reside, you have the opportunity (and in many cases the code obligation) to address air sealing at the same time.

**Measured energy savings from siding replacement with proper air sealing in Portland-area homes:**

|---|---|---|

| 1970s T1-11 with failed caulk, no WRB | $600–$900/yr | $6,000–$9,000 |
| Pre-1960 cedar lap, original everything | $720–$1,100/yr | $7,200–$11,000 |

These numbers come from blower-door tests conducted before and after exterior envelope projects in the Portland metro. They're not manufacturer claims. They're field measurements.

### 3. Insurance Stability

Oregon home insurers are increasingly scrutinizing exterior condition during underwriting. Visible paint failure, exposed wood substrate, and deteriorated caulk are all flags that can trigger:
- Premium increases of 8–15%
- Exclusions for water damage on specific elevations
- Non-renewal at policy anniversary

On a $2,400/year homeowner's policy, a 12% surcharge costs $288/year — $2,880 over 10 years. Maintaining your exterior eliminates this risk entirely.

### 4. Avoided Emergency Costs

Deferred siding maintenance doesn't fail gracefully. It fails in storms. A wind event that would cause zero damage to properly maintained siding can peel failed paint, open lapping joints, and drive water behind improperly sealed trim on a neglected house.

Emergency board-up, tarp, and temporary weatherproofing after storm damage to deteriorated siding: $800–$2,500 per event. The average Portland home with 5+ years of deferred exterior maintenance experiences 1–2 such events before the owner commits to a full project.

## The Portland Code Factor

Portland's Title 24 stormwater overlay doesn't directly regulate siding. But it intersects with exterior projects in two ways:

1. **Lead paint abatement.** Homes built before 1978 (most of Alameda qualifies) require RRP-certified contractors for any work that disturbs painted surfaces. This adds $1,200–$3,000 to the project cost but is non-negotiable under federal and state law. Factor it into your ROI calculation — it's real cost, but it's also real liability protection.

2. **Tree Code Title 11.50.** Portland's canopy preservation rules mean you generally cannot remove the mature trees that are accelerating your siding deterioration. The Douglas fir dropping needles into your lapping joints and the big leaf maple generating the moss pressure on your north elevation — they're staying. Your maintenance schedule must account for permanent biological load, not a temporary condition.

The 2026 ORSC energy envelope requirements mean that a siding replacement project in Portland triggers insulation and air-barrier upgrades. On a pre-1960 home, this can add $4,000–$8,000 to the siding project but also doubles the energy savings line in your ROI calculation. The code makes the project more expensive *and* more valuable. Net effect: ROI stays positive, payback accelerates.

## What Drives the Price in Beaverton

Three factors move your gutter cleaning cost above or below the baseline.

### 1. Roof Height and Pitch

Single-story ranch homes common in Cedar Hills near Tualatin Hills Nature Park are the most affordable to service. Two-story homes with 8/12 or steeper pitches require harness systems, longer ladder setups, and more time. The labor premium is real — it's a safety requirement, not an upsell.

### 2. Canopy Type and Debris Load

Beaverton's tree profile is mixed deciduous and conifer. The October-through-January window is the worst. Big-leaf maple drops broad, wet leaves that mat flat in the gutter trough. Douglas fir needles filter through guard openings and accumulate in downspout elbows.

A home on SW Murray Boulevard with three mature maples overhanging the roofline will cost more to clean than an identical home two blocks away with no canopy. Debris volume dictates labor time. Labor time dictates price.

### 3. Gutter Condition and Access

Gutters that haven't been cleaned in 2+ years often need additional work: resealing leaking miters, resetting popped hangers, clearing compacted decomposed material that standard scooping won't remove. If your fascia board shows water staining or soft spots, that's a separate repair scope — but we'll flag it during the cleaning.

## Why Beaverton Soil Makes This Urgent

Beaverton sits in the Tualatin Valley. The dominant soil series is silty clay loam. This soil has poor subsurface drainage — water doesn't percolate through it quickly. It sits. It saturates. It pushes.

When a clogged gutter overflows and sheets water directly against your foundation wall, that water doesn't drain away like it would in sandy soil. It accumulates in the backfill zone around your footing, saturates the clay, and generates hydrostatic pressure against the wall.

$$P_{\text{hydrostatic}} = \rho \cdot g \cdot h$$

At just 3 feet of saturated clay depth, the lateral pressure is enough to push water through hairline cracks in poured concrete — and to widen those cracks over freeze-thaw cycles.

The average foundation repair in the Tualatin Valley runs $8,000 to $22,000 depending on scope. Slab mudjacking starts around $1,500. Interior French drain systems run $6,000 to $12,000.

A $200 gutter cleaning prevents the conditions that lead to those invoices.

## The 2026 Code Standard

The 2026 Oregon Residential Specialty Code (ORSC) requires roof drainage systems on all eaves where grading or soil conditions direct water toward the structure. Beaverton Development Code Chapter 60, governed by Clean Water Services stormwater standards, adds specific requirements for on-site stormwater management.

In practice, this means your downspouts must discharge to an approved system — not just splash onto the grade next to your foundation. If your downspouts currently terminate at grade within 4 feet of the wall, you're not just risking foundation damage — you may be out of compliance.

We check downspout termination points during every cleaning. It's part of the service, not an add-on.

## When to Schedule in Beaverton

The optimal gutter cleaning schedule for most Beaverton homes:

- **Late November** — after the first major leaf drop from deciduous canopy
- **Late January/Early February** — after the secondary debris cycle and before the heaviest rain months
- **Optional: Late June** — for homes with heavy conifer canopy (fir pollen catkins and seed debris)

Homes in Cedar Hills with mixed canopy should budget for two cleanings minimum. Three if you have mature Douglas fir within 20 feet of the roofline.

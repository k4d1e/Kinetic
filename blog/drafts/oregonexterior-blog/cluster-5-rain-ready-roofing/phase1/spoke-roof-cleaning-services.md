# 7 Signs Your Roof is Leaking (Before the Water Hits the Floor)

## What You See First

You notice a brown stain on your bedroom ceiling. Or a musty smell in the hallway closet. Or paint blistering on an interior wall that shouldn't be getting wet.

By the time water shows up inside your house, the leak has been active for weeks. Sometimes months. The damage path from roof surface to ceiling drywall passes through sheathing, underlayment, framing, insulation, and vapor barriers. Every layer absorbs water before the next one saturates. Your ceiling stain is the last domino, not the first.

The good news: your roof telegraphs problems long before they reach your living space. Seven signs — all visible from the ground or from a ladder at the eave line — reveal active or developing leaks. Roof cleaning services can address several of these. But only if you spot them early enough to intervene.

Here's what to look for, why the physics matter, and what each sign actually tells you about the failure layer.

## Sign 1: Granule Accumulation in Gutters

### What You See

Dark, sand-like granules collecting in your gutters or piling up at downspout outlets. Some granule loss is normal in the first year after installation. After that, increasing granule volume in the gutter is a leading indicator.

### The Physics

Asphalt shingles rely on ceramic-coated granules for two functions: UV protection and water shedding. The granule layer creates a micro-rough surface that breaks water tension and directs flow downslope. As granules detach — from moss root intrusion, thermal cycling, or simple age — the exposed asphalt substrate becomes hydrophilic. It attracts and holds water instead of shedding it.

In Portland's Mount Tabor neighborhood, the heavy canopy of Douglas fir and big leaf maple around Mt. Tabor Park creates persistent shade conditions that accelerate biological granule stripping. Moss rhizoids — the root-like structures that anchor moss to surfaces — physically pry granules from the asphalt matrix. Each moss colony that establishes on your shingle surface is mechanically removing the layer that keeps water out.

### What It Means

Granule loss is Layer 1 failure. Your primary shedding surface is degrading. Roof cleaning services at this stage — soft-wash treatment, moss removal, zinc strip installation — can halt the progression. Wait another season, and you're into underlayment territory.

## Sign 2: Dark Streaks or Staining on the Roof Surface

### What You See

Black or dark green streaks running vertically down the roof plane. Homeowners often mistake these for dirt. They're not.

### The Physics

The black streaks are *Gloeocapsa magma* — a cyanobacterium that feeds on the limestone filler in asphalt shingles. The dark pigment is a UV shield the organism produces to protect itself. The green streaks are algae colonies, often mixed with early-stage moss.

Both organisms retain moisture against the shingle surface. In a climate with 200+ days of measurable precipitation, that moisture retention is continuous. The shingle never fully dries between rain events. This creates an accelerated aging environment: the shingle experiences the equivalent of 3-5 years of UV and moisture exposure for every calendar year of biological colonization.

Portland's soils — the Portland Hills Silt over Missoula Flood deposits that define most of the city's geology — contribute to this problem. The silt particles that wind carries from exposed ground surfaces provide mineral nutrition for roof-dwelling organisms. Your roof isn't just collecting rain. It's collecting soil.

### What It Means

Biological staining without granule loss is pre-failure. The damage is happening but hasn't yet compromised the shedding surface. This is the ideal window for roof cleaning services — the intervention cost is lowest and the recovery is complete.

## Sign 3: Lifted, Curled, or Buckled Shingle Edges

### What You See

Shingle edges that curl upward, corners that lift away from the course below, or a wavy/buckled appearance across the shingle field.

### The Physics

Shingles are composite structures: fiberglass mat, asphalt binder, granule surface. When moisture penetrates the shingle from the bottom — via condensation from inadequate attic ventilation — the fiberglass mat absorbs water and expands. The granule-coated top surface doesn't. Differential expansion curls the shingle upward at the edges.

Buckling happens when sheathing beneath the shingle absorbs moisture and expands. The shingle follows the substrate. In both cases, the curl or buckle opens the horizontal lap between shingle courses. That lap overlap is the primary defense against wind-driven rain. Once it opens, water enters at angles well below the design threshold.

Oregon's prevailing southwest winter winds push rain at 15-45 degrees from vertical. A 1/4-inch shingle curl converts the horizontal lap from a waterproof overlap into a funnel.

### What It Means

Curling and buckling signal moisture intrusion from below (ventilation failure) or shingle delamination from age and UV exposure. Roof cleaning services won't fix this — it's a structural condition. But a cleaning inspection often catches it before it escalates to active leaking.

## Sign 4: Rust Stains or Corrosion at Flashing Points

### What You See

Orange or brown rust streaks running down from chimney flashing, sidewall step flashing, pipe boots, or skylight frames.

### The Physics

Flashing metals — typically galvanized steel or aluminum — rely on a protective oxide layer to resist corrosion. When that layer is breached by mechanical damage (tree branches, foot traffic) or chemical attack (acidic moss runoff, tannic acid from cedar debris), the base metal oxidizes.

But the rust stain itself isn't the problem. It's the symptom. Corrosion at a flashing joint means the seal between the flashing and the adjacent surface is compromised. Water doesn't need a big opening. A 0.5mm gap at a step flashing joint lets water migrate behind the wall cladding via capillary action.

The capillary pressure at a 0.5mm gap is approximately:

\[
P_c = \frac{2 \times 0.073 \times \cos(20°)}{0.0005} \approx 274 \text{ Pa}
\]

That's enough to drive water 28mm (over an inch) uphill against gravity. Inside a wall cavity, that inch is the difference between flashing drainage and wall rot.

### What It Means

Flashing corrosion is Layer 3 failure — the most common leak source in Oregon homes over 15 years old. Roof cleaning services that include flashing inspection catch this early. Without inspection, this failure mode is invisible until drywall damage appears.

## Sign 5: Gutter Overflow Marks on Fascia

### What You See

Dark vertical streaks on the fascia board below your gutters. These appear after rain events and indicate water spilling over the gutter edge.

### The Physics

Gutters have a design flow rate based on their cross-section, slope, and outlet diameter. When biological debris — moss, leaf litter, fir needles from the Douglas fir canopy — reduces the effective gutter cross-section, flow rate drops. Water backs up. It overflows at the front edge, runs down the fascia, and — critically — also backs up behind the gutter into the sub-fascia and the lowest course of shingles.

The sub-fascia board is the structural member that supports your gutter. In most Oregon homes built before 2000, this is untreated dimensional lumber. It absorbs water readily. Once saturated, it wicks moisture into the rafter tails and the sheathing edge — the bottom 12 inches of your roof deck.

This is how a $15 gutter cleaning problem becomes a $3,000 fascia and sheathing replacement. The gutter didn't fail. The maintenance interval did.

### What It Means

Overflow marks mean your gutter capacity is compromised. This is the intersection of roof cleaning services and gutter maintenance — two disciplines that should never be separated in the PNW. Clean the roof without cleaning the gutters and you've just moved the debris downstream into a system that's already undersized.

## Sign 6: Daylight or Staining Visible in Attic

### What You See

Small points of light visible through the roof deck when the attic is dark. Or dark stains on the underside of the sheathing — especially at valleys, hips, and around penetrations (plumbing vents, exhaust fans).

### The Physics

Daylight through the decking means the waterproofing layers above that point have failed completely. The shingle is gone, the underlayment is torn, and direct exterior exposure exists. Even a pinhole allows rain intrusion, but more importantly, it creates a convective pathway — warm interior air exits, pulling moisture-laden exterior air in during temperature reversals.

Dark staining without daylight is subtler. It indicates moisture migration through the roof assembly that condenses on the cool decking surface. The 2026 ORSC ventilation requirements — 1:150 net free area ratio — exist specifically to prevent this. Inadequate ventilation traps moisture in the attic. That moisture condenses on the coldest surface available: the underside of the roof deck.

In the Mount Tabor area, where many homes date to the 1910s-1940s, original construction often has no soffit venting at all. The attic relies entirely on passive end-wall vents or a single turbine vent. These are thermally driven — they work when it's hot. During Oregon's cool, damp winter, they move almost no air. Condensation accumulates all season.

### What It Means

Attic staining is Layer 4 failure — ventilation. It's the most overlooked cause of roof-related damage in Oregon because the symptom (attic condensation) doesn't look like a "leak." But the sheathing damage is identical. Wet decking rots at the same rate whether the water came from outside or condensed from inside.

## Sign 7: Sagging or Soft Spots on the Roof Surface

### What You See

An area of the roof plane that appears to dip or sag between rafters. Or when walking the roof (which we recommend leaving to professionals), a section that feels spongy underfoot.

### The Physics

Sagging means the sheathing — typically 1/2-inch or 7/16-inch OSB (oriented strand board) — has lost structural integrity from prolonged moisture exposure. OSB is particularly vulnerable because it's an engineered wood product bonded with adhesive. When moisture penetrates the wax coating, the wood strands swell and the adhesive bond fails. The panel loses its shear strength and deflects under load.

This is end-stage failure. The shedding surface, the underlayment, and the sheathing are all compromised. The moisture source may be from above (failed shingles/flashing), from below (ventilation failure), or both.

### What It Means

Soft spots require immediate professional assessment. This is not a roof cleaning services issue — it's a structural repair. But it's included here because every Sign 7 started as a Sign 1, 2, or 3 that went unaddressed for too long.

## The Timeline: How Fast Signs Progress in Oregon

In a dry climate, you might get 2-3 years between Sign 1 (granule loss) and Sign 7 (structural failure). In Oregon's sustained-moisture environment, that timeline compresses dramatically:

- **Sign 1 to Sign 2** (granule loss to biological colonization): 6-12 months
- **Sign 2 to Sign 3** (colonization to shingle damage): 12-24 months
- **Sign 3 to Sign 5** (shingle damage to gutter compromise): Often simultaneous — debris from degrading shingles clogs gutters
- **Sign 4** (flashing failure): Independent timeline, but accelerated by biological acid from Signs 1-2
- **Sign 6** (ventilation/condensation): Ongoing from day one if ventilation is inadequate
- **Sign 7** (structural): 24-48 months after any unaddressed failure reaches the decking

In a neighborhood like Mount Tabor, where the tree canopy from Mt. Tabor Park keeps roof surfaces shaded and damp year-round, these timelines compress further. We've documented cases where granule loss to structural compromise occurred in under 30 months on north-facing roof planes.

## What to Do With This Information

Walk your property this weekend. You don't need to get on the roof. A pair of binoculars and a walk around the perimeter will reveal Signs 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5. A flashlight in the attic will reveal Sign 6. Sign 7 is visible from the street if it's advanced.

Any single sign is actionable. Two or more signs in combination — especially Signs 1+5 (granule loss plus gutter overflow) or Signs 3+6 (shingle damage plus attic condensation) — indicate a compounding failure that accelerates with every rain event.

Roof cleaning services address Signs 1, 2, and 5 directly. Signs 3 and 4 require repair-level intervention. Signs 6 and 7 require structural work. The cost difference between cleaning-level and structural-level repair is typically 5-10x.

The math is simple: invest in regular roof cleaning services, or pay for the compounded consequences later.

## The Hidden Costs: What's Behind Your Siding

Here's where Oregon pricing diverges from national averages. Our 154-day rainy season and heavy biological load from Douglas fir, big leaf maple, and western red cedar canopy mean moisture damage behind siding is common. Portland's Tree Code (Title 11.50) preserves that canopy, which is good for the urban forest and tough on exterior walls.

### Sheathing Repair/Replacement

| Scope | Added Cost/sq ft | When It Applies |
|---|---|---|
| **Spot repair** (under 10% of wall area) | $2.00–$4.00 | Localized rot at window sills, inside corners, or near downspouts |
| **Partial replacement** (10–40%) | $4.00–$6.00 | Widespread but not structural. Common on north and west exposures. |
| **Full sheathing replacement** | $6.00–$8.00 | Structural compromise. Soft, black, or delaminated OSB/plywood across large areas. |

### Drainage Plane Retrofit

The 2026 Oregon Residential Specialty Code requires drainage provisions behind all cladding in Climate Zone 4C. If your existing wall has housewrap stapled flat to sheathing with no drainage gap, you're adding:

| Component | Added Cost/sq ft |
|---|---|
| **Drainable housewrap** (crinkle-type WRB) | $0.50–$1.00 |
| **Drainage mat** (e.g., Benjamin Obdyke HydroGap) | $0.75–$1.25 |
| **Furring strips** (1x3 or 1x4 over WRB, creates 3/4" rainscreen gap) | $1.00–$2.00 |

Furring strips are the most reliable drainage solution and are required when installing over continuous rigid foam insulation.

### Insulation Upgrades

If you're opening walls anyway, adding continuous exterior insulation is cost-effective at the margin:

| Insulation Type | Added Cost/sq ft |
|---|---|
| **1" rigid foam (R-5)** | $1.50–$2.50 |
| **1.5" rigid foam (R-7.5)** | $2.00–$3.50 |
| **Mineral wool (1.5", R-6)** | $2.50–$4.00 |

The 2026 ORSC requires continuous insulation for new construction. For re-siding, it's not mandatory unless you're replacing more than 25% of the wall assembly — but the energy savings and condensation prevention make it worth considering.

## Portland Permitting

Siding replacement in Portland requires a permit when:
- You're changing the cladding material (e.g., vinyl to Hardie)
- You're altering the wall assembly (adding insulation, changing WRB)
- You're repairing more than 25% of the structural sheathing

Permit fees for residential re-siding in Portland run $300–$800 depending on scope. Inspections include WRB/flashing verification before cladding goes on.

Portland's Title 24 stormwater overlay may apply if your project changes how water drains from the wall to grade. In Foster-Powell — built on Missoula Flood alluvium with variable percolation — concentrated drainage at the foundation needs to be addressed.

## When to Replace vs. When to Repair

**Replace when:**
- Moisture meter readings above 19% in multiple locations
- Sheathing is soft or delaminated behind siding
- Siding material is discontinued (no matching replacement available)
- You're upgrading the wall assembly to meet 2026 code for an addition or remodel

**Repair/maintain when:**
- Damage is localized (under 10% of wall area)
- The substrate is sound (screwdriver test passes)
- The existing WRB and flashing are intact
- Paint or stain is failing but the material isn't

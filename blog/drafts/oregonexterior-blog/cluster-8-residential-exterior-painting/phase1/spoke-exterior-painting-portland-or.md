# 2026 Portland & Salem Exterior Painting Cost Guide

Exterior painting in Portland OR runs $3.50–$7.00 per square foot in 2026.

The price gap isn't markup. It's physics.

The $3.50 quote skips moisture testing, uses one-coat coverage, and applies acrylic over dirty substrate. You'll repaint in 3 years.

The $7.00 quote tests moisture, primes properly, repairs substrate, and builds a two-coat system engineered to survive 40+ inches of annual rainfall. You'll repaint in 12–15 years.

This is your cost breakdown for Portland, Beaverton, Lake Oswego, West Linn, and Salem — what you're paying for, what moves the price, and the math on when quality pays for itself.

## What You're Actually Paying For

Let's break down the cost per square foot and what it buys you.

### $3.50–$4.50/sq ft: The "Refresh Coat"

**What's included:**
- Pressure wash (1,200–1,500 PSI for wood)
- Spot scraping of loose paint
- One-coat application (standard acrylic, builder-grade)
- No moisture testing
- No primer (unless bare wood is exposed)

**What's skipped:**
- Moisture testing (required under 2026 ORSC for warranty compliance)
- Full surface prep (feathering edges, sanding glossy surfaces)
- Caulking repair (seams, penetrations, butt joints)
- Substrate repair (rotted wood, failed trim)

**When it works:**  
If your existing paint is in good condition (no peeling, minimal chalking, no substrate moisture), and you're repainting within 5–7 years of the last paint job.

**When it fails:**  
If there's any substrate moisture, failed caulking, or surface contamination. The new coat delaminates within 18–36 months.

**Typical project in Irvington (Portland):** 1,800 sq ft Craftsman with wood lap siding near Irving Park. Douglas fir and big leaf maple canopy creates 60% shade. North-facing walls hold moisture longer. A one-coat refresh job fails by winter 2028.

### $5.00–$6.00/sq ft: The Engineered System

**What's included:**
- Pressure wash + 48-hour dry time
- Moisture testing (digital pin-type meter, documented readings)
- Full scraping and sanding (remove all loose/peeling paint, feather edges)
- Caulking repair (polyurethane or tripolymer at all seams, penetrations, joints)
- Spot-prime all bare wood and repairs (alkyd or shellac-based primer)
- Two-coat system (premium 100% acrylic)
- Clean-up and final inspection

**What's skipped:**
- Extensive substrate repair (unless priced separately)
- Premium coatings (elastomeric, designer finishes)

**When it works:**  
For any wood or fiber cement siding where you want 10–15 years of durability. This is the price point where you're buying physics, not just color.

**Typical project in West Linn:** 2,400 sq ft two-story near Mary S. Young Park. Basalt hardpan and perched water tables mean drainage is critical. Proper caulking and moisture testing catch problems before they become $30K siding replacements.

### $6.50–$7.00+/sq ft: Premium + Problem-Solving

**What's included:**
- Everything in the $5–$6 tier
- Substrate repair (replace rotted boards, re-flash penetrations)
- Premium coatings (elastomeric for stucco, high-solids acrylic for extreme exposures)
- Multi-story scaffolding access (gables, dormers, complex rooflines)
- Historic detail work (Craftsman trim, decorative brackets)

**When it's required:**  
When you're solving structural problems, not just repainting. If your screwdriver sinks into the siding, you're here.

**Typical project in Lake Oswego:** 3,000 sq ft 1920s Craftsman near George Rogers Park. Iron-rich basalt and dense clay create drainage issues. Heavy canopy (Douglas fir, western red cedar) drives aggressive moss colonization. Rotted fascia and trim boards add $4,000 in repair before painting starts.

## The Variables That Move Price

### 1. Substrate Condition

**Clean, dry, stable paint:** Base pricing applies.

**Chalking or mild peeling:** Add 10–15% for extra prep (sanding, additional primer).

**Moderate peeling or substrate moisture:** Add 20–30% for extensive scraping, moisture remediation, and repair.

**Rotted wood or failed caulking:** Add $1,500–$5,000+ for board replacement and structural repair before painting.

### 2. Access and Complexity

**Single-story ranch:** Base pricing.

**Two-story with standard ladder access:** Add 10–15%.

**Multi-story with gables, dormers, or complex rooflines:** Add 20–30% for scaffolding rental and labor.

**Heavy landscaping or tight access:** Add 5–10% for extra setup time.

### 3. Trim and Detail Work

**Modern smooth lap with minimal trim:** Base pricing.

**Craftsman-era detailing (brackets, corbels, multi-piece trim):** Add 15–25% for hand-brushing and detail work.

**Historically-designated homes:** Add 30–50% for compliance with historic preservation standards (Portland Landmarks Commission, Oregon SHPO).

### 4. Season and Demand

**Summer (June–August):** Peak demand. Quotes run 10–15% higher. Book 8–12 weeks in advance.

**Spring/Fall (April–May, September–October):** Moderate demand. Pricing at baseline. Weather risk increases (dew, rain delays).

**Winter (November–March):** Off-season. Quotes 10–15% lower, but Oregon's 150-day wet season makes quality work nearly impossible. Any painter willing to spray in January is cutting corners.

### 5. Geographic Micro-Pricing

**Portland (Laurelhurst, Irvington, Sellwood-Moreland):** Baseline. Heavy canopy and urban density.

**Beaverton, Tigard:** Add 5%. Tualatin Valley silty clay loam holds moisture longer. More substrate prep required.

**Lake Oswego, West Linn:** Add 10%. Premium neighborhoods, historic homes, tighter access, more detail work.

**Salem:** Subtract 10–15% on labor, but add back 5–10% for Willamette Silt soil issues. Seasonal shrink-swell cracks caulk joints faster.

**Vancouver:** Baseline to -5%. Labor slightly lower, but Columbia River flood deposits and seasonal water tables increase substrate moisture risk.

## The 2026 Code Updates: What Changed

The 2026 Oregon Residential Specialty Code (ORSC) added two requirements that affect pricing:

**1. Moisture testing before application**  
Any wood substrate must be tested with a digital moisture meter. Readings above 19% void manufacturer warranties. Contractors must document and provide readings to homeowners.

**Cost impact:** $150–$300 per project (meter rental, labor, documentation). Quality contractors already did this. Budget contractors now have to, or they're violating code.

**2. VOC compliance for Portland Title 24 and Vancouver air quality**  
Low-VOC or waterborne alkyd formulations required. Traditional oil-based primers and topcoats exceed 250 g/L limit.

**Cost impact:** Premium low-VOC alkyd costs $10–$20 more per gallon than traditional oil-based. Adds $200–$400 to a typical project.

## When to Pull the Trigger

Paint when you see these conditions:

**1. Chalking** — Rub your hand on the siding. Powdery residue means the binder's failing. 1–2 years left.

**2. Peeling or alligatoring** — Paint lifting in sheets or showing reptile-scale cracking. Adhesion or cohesion failure. Repaint now.

**3. Mildew staining** — Black/green streaks on north-facing walls. Paint permeability has degraded. Biological growth is accelerating substrate decay.

**4. Failed caulking** — Gaps at seams, penetrations, or butt joints. Water is entering the wall assembly.

**5. 7–10 years since last paint** — Even if it looks good, you're in the repaint window for wood siding in Oregon.
